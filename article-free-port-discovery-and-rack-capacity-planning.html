Planning your next rack without guessing at free ports

Teams plan capacity better when they can see which ports are actually open, not which ones a spreadsheet says should be.

On this page

The guessing game Why the spreadsheet quietly goes wrong What guessing wrong costs Planning from what's actually open

Capacity planning usually starts with a spreadsheet's confident answer to a question nobody actually walked over and checked: how many ports are really free?

The guessing game most teams play

Planners trust the record because checking every port physically takes too long to do for every project. That's a reasonable trade-off, right up until the record turns out to be wrong on install day.

“A free port on paper and a free port in the rack are two different claims, and only one of them will actually take a cable.”

Why the spreadsheet quietly goes wrong

The record goes wrong in both directions, quietly:

  • A port freed up months ago is still marked in-use.
  • A port marked free already has something plugged into it, unlogged.
  • Neither mistake gets caught until someone shows up with a cable.
1 in 4

ports flagged “available” in a sample sweep turned out to already be occupied.

What guessing wrong actually costs

It's a failed install day. It's an emergency reshuffle nobody planned for. It's a rollout that slips because the port everyone was counting on wasn't actually there.

In plain terms

It's the difference between reserving a parking spot from a map and actually driving up to find a car already sitting in it.

Planning from what's actually open

Use a current physical sweep as the starting point for the next rack plan, instead of trusting a spreadsheet that hasn't been checked against reality since it was written.
